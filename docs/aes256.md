# AES-256 voice and web CPS

P25 Phase 1 conventional voice uses ALGID 0x84, a 16-bit KID and AES-256 OFB following TIA-102.AAAD-A. DMR conventional voice use the Motorola FID 0x10 PI layout (algorithm 0x25, 8-bit key ID). This does not add AES to NXDN, P25 Phase 2, data packets, or trunked transmission.

Four locally provisioned key slots live in the open-firmware EEPROM image at 0x7C00. A versioned 16-byte header is followed by four 96-byte CRC-protected entries. Each entry holds the KID, 32-byte key, a 16-byte browser-generated random seed, owning chip UID, a provisioning marker and reserved bytes. Channel byte 0x32 bits 0-4 store 0 (disabled) or 17-20 (slots 1-4); all other bits are preserved. Only explicit zero permits clear TX; any invalid nonzero selector denies TX. The web CPS Radio tab edits slots using masked inputs and channels select a slot, in the CPS or in the radio's channel menu (the **AES-256** row offers only slots that hold a key; the menu reads the key ID, never the key). The firmware reports `DV_AES` in its feature list; the web CPS writes channels with a slot only to a build that reports it. AES keys are excluded from CSV and YAML; binary codeplug backups contain them and must be kept private.

The cipher primitive is tiny-AES-c, pinned and vendored under the Unlicense. DMR IV expansion and keystream ordering follow the MIT dmrgo library; attribution is retained. P25 uses published protocol vectors. No radio firmware source is used.

Before encrypted TX, the radio reserves 4096 superframes from a persistent global high-water counter in the settings journal. It derives the initial MI by expanding the browser seed with the AES key and chip UID, rejecting zero initial states, then jumping the protocol LFSR by the reserved sequence offset. The counter is outside the CPS image and cannot be rolled back by ordinary codeplug writes. Encrypted voice starts only after settings_flush succeeds. A call cannot exceed its reservation. A missing key, invalid selector, unsupported KID, failed reservation, or unavailable chip UID denies encrypted TX without clear fallback. The 32-bit global sequence budget prevents local wrap. A first-use owner marker in the key entry is journaled before the first reservation; if settings were reset while the key entry remains bound to this chip, TX is denied until re-provisioning with a fresh browser seed. Power loss between marker and counter writes safely denies TX. Full-flash rollback, or restoring a provisioning snapshot after resetting settings, cannot be prevented without hardware rollback protection: rekey in that case. Changing any key or KID generates a new random seed. Unchanged entries round-trip byte-for-byte.

DMR's 32-bit MI cannot offer fleet-wide collision resistance with independently randomized transmitters sharing a key. Two independently seeded 4096-superframe reservations overlap with probability approximately 8191/(2^32-1), and risk accumulates over calls. For confidentiality-sensitive use, allocate a different key/KID per transmitter; do not claim shared-key fleet confidentiality. This limitation is intrinsic to this Motorola-compatible PI format. P25 uses a 64-bit MI, which has a much larger space.

The channel selects the TX key slot. RX requires valid encryption synchronization and finds the matching KID among the four provisioned slots. Duplicate KIDs are rejected by the CPS. Missing/unsupported PI/ALGID is muted. P25 LDU2 carries the next superframe MI; late entry waits for the next LDU1. DMR late entry without a PI header is muted; embedded-IV recovery is outside this first version. Missing bursts advance cipher position by their known sequence position, and uncertain discontinuities clear synchronization. Clear voice behavior remains available on channels without AES. OFB has no authentication: a different key with the same KID cannot be reliably detected, and this feature cannot promise authenticated audio or metadata protection.

Validation covers AES-256 and OFB known answers, P25 MI/IV and published keystream vectors, DMR cross-checks against the pinned independent library, voice/FEC round trips, damaged/missing synchronization, key selection, journal-write failure, reboot reservations, read/transmit/edit/write without reservation rollback, CPS round trips and validation, and feature-off builds. Firmware flash, RAM and stack limits must pass. Actual interoperability and RF timing remain untested until an authorized hardware test.

## P25 receive: what was checked against what

A tester with several Motorola radios on one key and key ID reported that the DM-32UV did not decode them. The cause is not known: no Motorola capture or radio has been available here. What was checked, independently of the firmware's own vectors:

| Check | Against | Result |
|---|---|---|
| IV (MI and its LFSR expansion), AES-256 OFB keystream, discarded first block, byte offset of each of the 18 IMBE frames of a superframe; 300 random keys and MIs | OP25's own `op25_crypt_aes` (commit pinned in `sources.lock.json`), compiled and called by `tests/protocol/test_p25.py` | identical; `tests/test_aes.c` also holds OP25's 18 masks for one key, which CI runs |
| HDU, LDU1 and LDU2 of an AES call with ALGID, KID and MI, 60 random calls, half with a nonzero ninth MI byte | OP25's `construct_hdu`, `construct_ldu1`, `construct_ldu2` | byte-identical |
| Which MI each superframe uses (HDU for the first, each LDU2's for the next) and which fields decide decryption (ALGID, KID, MI) | OP25's `p25p1_fdma.cc` | the same |
| A Motorola or other radio over the air | | **not tested** |

OP25 is an independent implementation of the standard. Agreement with it shows the firmware reads the standard as OP25 does, not what a given radio sends.

Receive is now as tolerant as OP25 where it was stricter for no cryptographic reason, because OP25 decrypts from the encryption sync alone:

- The ninth byte of the 72-bit MI field is ignored (the IV uses 64 bits); a radio that sends it nonzero used to be muted.
- The link control's protected bit and format no longer decide. Once a valid header or LDU2 sync armed the call, an LDU1 whose link control says the call is unprotected, is damaged, or is of a kind not read here (another format or manufacturer) is still decrypted; the call keeps the talkgroup the header gave it.
- A unit ID is any 24-bit value; one above 9,999,999 used to keep a call from playing, clear or encrypted.

It stays stricter than OP25 where a wrong guess would play noise: the MI must not be zero; an LDU1 whose link control names another group or caller than the call's is not decrypted with its keystream; and a call with neither a talkgroup from the header nor a readable link control does not play (`AES: bad link control`). A damaged ESS in an LDU2 still costs that LDU2 and the next superframe (OP25 carries on by stepping the MI itself).

## Setting up a channel to hear an AES-256 call

1. Load the key in the web CPS **Radio** tab. The **KID is decimal** there (1-65535). Motorola's key tools, OP25 and most scanners show it in hex: hex `1234` is `4660`.
2. Select the slot on the channel (CPS, or the **AES-256** row of the radio's channel menu). Without a slot the channel decrypts nothing, whatever keys are loaded. The key used is the one stored under the KID in the call's header, in any of the four slots.
3. The call decrypts from its header. One heard late waits for the next LDU2 encryption sync, then plays from the following superframe (up to about 0.4 s of speech is lost).
4. The channel's NAC and talkgroup apply as for clear calls (Hear All and the digital monitor lift the talkgroup filter). A call on another talkgroup is not played even when it decrypts, and shows its TG and ID rather than `Encrypted`.

## What the radio shows

Under `Encrypted TG n` (the call is heard and muted) a second line says why:

| Line | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| `Pick an AES-256 key` | the channel has no key slot | select a slot on the channel |
| `KID 4660: no key` | AES-256 call, but no slot holds that KID | load the key, or fix the KID (decimal) |
| `ALG 81 not supported` | an algorithm other than AES-256; the number is the ALGID in hex (81 DES-OFB, AA ADP) | nothing: only AES-256 is decrypted |
| `AES: waiting for sync` | the key is there; no usable encryption sync yet | wait for the next transmission |
| `AES: bad link control` | the call is armed, but this LDU1's link control is unusable and the header gave no talkgroup to carry on with | report it |

While a decrypted call plays, `KID 4660: wrong key?` replaces the caller line. With a wrong key (or keystream) about one clean frame in five carries a pitch the vocoder cannot use; with the right key none does. The radio usually flags it within one to three superframes. It is a hint: audio is not muted, and a signal too weak to have clean frames is never flagged.

## What to report from a failing test

- The second line under `Encrypted TG n`, or whether `wrong key?` appears while the call plays.
- The radio model and firmware, the key ID as the radio and the CPS show it (and in which base), and which channel mode and slot.
- Whether the call is heard from the start or only after a pause, and whether anything is heard at all.
- A `wrong key?` with a key known to be right, or `bad link control` or `waiting for sync` that never clears, points at a difference in what that radio transmits. Say which radio and software version sent it; an OP25 or SDR capture of the HDU and first LDU1 and LDU2 (`algid`, `keyid`, `mi`, the LC) settles it.
